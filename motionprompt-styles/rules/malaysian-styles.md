# Malaysian styles: accuracy and respect

Applies to: `malaysian-heritage`, `festive-raya`, `merdeka`, `deepavali`, `chinese-new-year`, `batik-fashion`, `kopitiam`, `pasar-malam`, `kampung-sunset`, `borneo-rainforest`, `nostalgia-90s`, `map-journey`. When unsure of a fact, ask the user or keep the element generic rather than inventing detail.

## General

- Write in the language the user used (Bahasa Melayu or English). Do not "correct" a user's spelling of a brand or name.
- **Respect first**: religious and national symbols are drawn accurately or not at all; do not use them as decoration in a way that could offend (no text over the flag, no distorted flag, no sacred text as pattern).
- **Halal-safe defaults** for Malay-Muslim audiences: default foods and drinks to kopi, teh tarik, kaya toast, half-boiled eggs, chicken or beef satay, apam balik, air sirap; do not add pork or alcohol imagery unless the user asks and the context is right.
- Motifs are drawn as clean vector shapes and kept respectful; no cartoon caricatures of people.

## Jalur Gemilang (Malaysian flag), `merdeka`

- Ratio 1:2. **14 equal horizontal stripes, alternating red and white, starting with red at the top**, ending with white.
- **Dark blue canton** in the top-left covering the **upper 8 stripes** in height and **half the flag's width** (from the hoist to the middle).
- In the canton: a **yellow crescent opening toward the right (the fly)** and a **yellow 14-pointed star** to its right, centred vertically.
- Colours: red `#cc0001`, white `#ffffff`, blue `#010066`, yellow `#ffcc00`. Never recolour with brand colours.
- Meaning (for captions if asked): 14 stripes and 14 points = equal status of the 13 states and the federal territories; blue = unity, yellow = royal colour of the Malay rulers.
- Dates: Hari Merdeka 31 August (1957, Malaya), Hari Malaysia 16 September (1963). Skyline shapes: Petronas Twin Towers (two towers with a sky bridge and spires), KL Tower (thin shaft with a pod), as simple dark silhouettes.
- Greetings: "Selamat Hari Merdeka", "Selamat Hari Malaysia". Do not invent the year's official slogan.

## Wau bulan (`malaysian-heritage`)

Kelantan crescent-moon kite: **wide symmetrical wings, a crescent-shaped tail, a decorated body with layered paper and floral cut-work, and a bow (busur) on top with tassels** (the bow hums in the wind). Draw it that way; strings trail to the ground.

## Batik (`malaysian-heritage`, `batik-fashion`)

- Canting = the wax pen (a small copper reservoir with a spout); wax lines are cream. Dye colours soak in after the wax outlines.
- Motifs: **bunga raya (hibiscus, the national flower)**, leaves, **awan larat** (flowing spirals and tendrils), clouds, isen dots (filler dots/patterns), fine crackle lines. Malaysian batik favours large bright florals and geometry (not figurative animals).
- Palette for the styles: indigo, magenta, turmeric, emerald, royal blue, coral on wax cream.

## Songket and pucuk rebung

Songket is hand-woven cloth with gold/silver metallic thread. **Pucuk rebung** = bamboo-shoot triangle motif (rows of triangles), used as a border. Panels are maroon or deep colours with gold patterns.

## Hari Raya Aidilfitri (`festive-raya`)

- Elements: ketupat (woven diamond rice pouches), pelita (bamboo oil lamps lit at night), lanterns, crescent moon, eight-point-star Islamic geometric patterns, kompang (frame drums), palm and mosque silhouettes, gold and emerald.
- **No Quranic text, no Arabic calligraphy of verses, and no people.** Geometry and lamps only. A mosque silhouette is a simple dome and minaret.
- Greetings: **"Selamat Hari Raya Aidilfitri"**, "Selamat Hari Raya", **"Maaf Zahir dan Batin"** (forgiveness, inside and out), "Salam Aidilfitri". Green packets are "duit raya" (only as an abstract object, no faces).

## Deepavali (`deepavali`)

- Elements: **kolam** (the South Indian floor pattern; rangoli in the north; in Malaysia "kolam" is common), clay **diyas** with painted bands, marigold garlands, gold sparkles, deep purple night.
- **No deities or religious figures**, and no people.
- Greetings: "Selamat Deepavali", "Happy Deepavali", "Deepavali greetings". Spelling in Malaysia is Deepavali.
- Music: tanpura drone, tabla, bansuri flute: instrumental only.

## Chinese New Year (`chinese-new-year`)

- Elements: red paper-cuts (flowers, clouds, scrolls), round red lanterns with gold tassels, plum blossom (mei), gold ingots and coins, red seal badges, mandarin oranges, red packets ("ang pow" is the common Malaysian term).
- Greetings: **"Gong Xi Fa Cai"** (Mandarin), "Selamat Tahun Baru Cina" (Malay), "Happy Chinese New Year". Characters only if the user provides them; **Ma Shan Zheng** brush style; the character 福 (fu) is customarily shown upright or inverted: keep it upright unless asked.
- Keep product claims gentle and honest (no medical or superlative claims). Colour: red and gold; avoid white/black dominance.

## Kopitiam, pasar malam, kampung

- **Kopitiam**: marble-top round tables, tile walls in green/cream, louvred windows, ceiling fans; kopi (with sweet condensed milk), teh tarik (pulled tea, an "mamak" tradition), kaya toast (coconut jam with butter), half-boiled eggs with soy sauce and pepper.
- **Pasar malam**: rows of stalls with striped canopies, string bulbs, satay on charcoal, apam balik (folded turnover pancake), drinks in plastic bags with a straw; prices in **RM** ("RM5", "3 for RM10").
- **Kampung house**: raised on tall stilts, steep pitched roof (attap or zinc), a porch/serambi with steps, open windows, coconut palms, paddy fields, a raised path (batas). "Balik kampung" = going back to the village.

## Borneo (`borneo-rainforest`)

- **Rhinoceros hornbill** (Sarawak's state bird): black plumage, **white tail with a black band**, large yellow bill with an **orange-red casque curving upward**.
- **Rafflesia**: huge parasitic flower, **five thick reddish petals with cream/pale spots**, on the forest floor (no leaves or stem of its own).
- Dipterocarps: very tall, straight, pale trunks with wide buttress roots. Morning mist and light shafts.

## Music and sound

Instrumental only (no chanting or vocals). Instruments to imitate: gendang, kompang and rebana (frame drums), gambus (plucked lute), seruling (bamboo flute), gong; Indian: tabla, tanpura, bansuri; Chinese: guzheng, woodblock, small cymbals. All are synthesised: see the presets `pentatonic-heritage`, `kompang-raya`, `joget`, `indian-drone`, `festive-pentatonic`, `east-asian-zither`.
